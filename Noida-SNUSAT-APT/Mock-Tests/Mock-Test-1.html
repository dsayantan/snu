Riya runs a 1,200 m obstacle course. On the flat stretches she runs at a steady 4 m/s. The rest of the course is made of hurdle sections, each 10 m long, and she spends a total of exactly 60 seconds crossing all the hurdle sections.

If she finishes in 4 minutes 30 seconds, how many hurdle sections does the course have?

Source: Predicted 2027, SNUSAT V12

How to see it

The hurdle time is given as a total, so subtract it; what is left is flat running at a known speed, which gives the flat distance.

Working

  1. Total time = 270 s; flat time = 270 − 60 = 210 s.
  2. Flat distance = 4 × 210 = 840 m.
  3. Hurdle distance = 1,200 − 840 = 360 m.
  4. Number of sections = 360 ÷ 10 = 36.
Answer — C — 36.
Common slip. Using 4 min 30 s as 430 s, or forgetting to take the hurdle time out before using the flat speed.

A highway project was estimated at Rs 12,000 crores, of which 25% was the cost of steel. Before construction began, the price of steel rose by 30%.

By how much did the estimated project cost rise because of the steel price alone?

Source: Predicted 2027, SNUSAT V13

How to see it

Only the steel share changes. Take 25% of the total, then 30% of that.

Working

  1. Steel cost = 25% of 12,000 = Rs 3,000 crores.
  2. Rise = 30% of 3,000 = Rs 900 crores.
  3. In one line: 12,000 × 0.25 × 0.30 = 900.
  4. 3,600 is 30% of the whole project; 3,900 is the new steel cost.
Answer — B — Rs 900 crores.
Common slip. Applying the 30% to the whole project cost.

A machine produces toys at the rate of t toys per hour, where t > 1. It takes 6 hours to produce an entire batch of toys. If the machine has already produced 4 toys, how many more hours are required to complete the entire batch?

Source: 2027 paper, SNUSAT Q1

How to see it

Work in toys first, hours last. The batch is a number of toys; turn the toys still owed back into hours by dividing by the rate.

Working

  1. Whole batch = rate × time = 6t toys.
  2. Still to make: 6t − 4 toys.
  3. Time for those = (6t − 4)/t = 6 − 4/t hours.
  4. Sanity check with t = 4: a 24-toy batch, 4 done, 20 left at 4 an hour is 5 hours, and 6 − 4/4 = 5. ✓
Answer — D — 6 − 4/t.
Common slip. Choosing B, which is the number of toys left, not the hours.

On a DIGITAL CALCULATOR, some numbers look like English words when the display is turned 180°. For example, entering 0.7734 appears as “hello”.

Which of the following numbers will form a meaningful English word when the display is turned 180°?

Source: 2026 paper, SNUSAT Q12

How to see it

Turning the display upside down does two things at once — it reverses the order and flips each glyph.

Working

  1. Seven-segment digits upside down: 0→O, 1→I, 2→Z, 3→E, 4→h, 5→S, 6→g, 7→L, 8→B, 9→G.
  2. Check the worked example: 0.7734 reversed is 4377.0 → h, E, L, L, O — “hELLO”. The rule is confirmed.
  3. 138 → reverse 831 → B E I — no.
  4. 508 → reverse 805 → B O S — no.
  5. 5537 → reverse 7355 → L E S S — a word.
  6. 7137 → reverse 7317 → L E I L — no.
Answer — C — 5537 reads LESS.
Common slip. Mapping the digits without reversing them. 5537 read left to right gives SSEL, which is nothing — the reversal is half the trick.

How to see it

Before fitting shapes, count colours. Any tile that repeats to make the pattern must have the same share of black pentagons as the pattern itself.

The line −(x + 2) = y does NOT pass through which quadrant?

Source: 2026 paper, SNUSAT Q13

How to see it

A line misses exactly one quadrant when it has a non-zero intercept; the sign of the slope and of the intercept say which.

Working

  1. −(x + 2) = y gives y = −x − 2: slope −1, y-intercept −2, x-intercept −2.
  2. Quadrant 1 needs x > 0 and y > 0. For x > 0, y = −x − 2 < −2, always negative.
  3. Q2: (−3, 1) lies on it. Q3: (−1, −1). Q4: (1, −3). All three are hit.
  4. So the line never enters Quadrant 1.
Answer — A — Quadrant 1.
Common slip. Losing the minus sign on the bracket. y = −x + 2 would miss Quadrant 3 instead, which is the distractor the question is built around.

The keyboard on Avinash's phone has a problem. When he taps a certain letter, sometimes a different letter altogether comes up on screen. Three of the errors are:

On tapping “L”, the screen displays “S”
On tapping “S”, the screen displays “P”
On tapping “P”, the screen displays “A”

Note that these are just a few instances, and there may be other errors as well.

If Avinash taps S, E, P, A, L in order, which of the following could his screen display?

Source: 2025 paper, SNUSAT Q11

How to see it

The taps are in a fixed order, so check the options position by position, not as anagrams.

Working

  1. Tap 1 is S, which can show S or P. Tap 2 is E. Tap 3 is P, which can show P or A. Tap 4 is A. Tap 5 is L, which can show L or S.
  2. LAPSE: needs L first — S cannot produce L. Out.
  3. PALES: P​A​L​E​S needs E in position 2, but it has A. Out.
  4. LEAPS: needs L first again. Out.
  5. PEALS: P (from S) · E (unchanged) · A (from P) · L (from A, allowed by “there may be other errors”) · S (from L). Every position is reachable.
Answer — D — PEALS.
Common slip. Treating the five options as anagrams of one another and hunting for the one with the right letters. All four have the same letters; only the order separates them.

What fraction of the total number of questions in the test were from algebra?

Source: 2027 paper, SNUSAT Q7

How to see it

A fraction of a fraction does not need the actual number; let it be n and it cancels.

Working

  1. Each type has n questions, so the test has 3n.
  2. Algebra = n/4 + n/3 = 7n/12.
  3. Fraction = (7n/12) / 3n = 7/36.
Answer — A — 7/36.
Common slip. Answering 7/12 — that is algebra as a fraction of one type's count, forgetting there are three types.

Aryan is competing in an 800 m cycling race, which consists of two types of sections:

  • Even road: on these smooth stretches Aryan rides at a constant speed of 5 m/s.
  • Uneven sections: each uneven section spans 6 m, and the total time Aryan spends crossing all these sections is exactly 50 seconds.

After every 200 m of the race, the number of uneven sections in the next 200 m is halved compared to the previous 200 m.

If Aryan completes the race in 2 minutes 54 seconds, what is the total number of uneven sections he crossed?

Source: 2027 paper, SNUSAT Q2

How to see it

The total time and the uneven time are both given, so the even-road time follows, and with it the even distance. The halving rule is only a consistency check.

Working

  1. Total time = 2 min 54 s = 174 s. Time on even road = 174 − 50 = 124 s.
  2. Even distance = 5 × 124 = 620 m, so the uneven sections cover 800 − 620 = 180 m.
  3. Number of sections = 180 / 6 = 30.
  4. Check against the halving rule: 16 + 8 + 4 + 2 = 30, so the four 200 m blocks can indeed hold them. ✓
Answer — D — 30.
Common slip. Trying to use the halving rule to find the count. It cannot fix the total on its own; the time data do.

The total surface area of a cube is s. Two such cubes are glued together face to face to form a cuboid.

What is the total surface area of the cuboid?

Source: Predicted 2027, SNUSAT V14

How to see it

Gluing removes the touching face from each cube, so two faces vanish. Write a face in terms of s.

Working

  1. One face = s/6 (a cube has six equal faces).
  2. The two cubes together have 2s; the two glued faces are lost.
  3. Area = 2s − 2(s/6) = 2s − s/3 = 5s/3.
  4. Check with side 1: two cubes 12, cuboid 1×1×2 has 2+4+4 = 10 = 5(6)/3. ✓
Answer — D — 2s − s/3.
Common slip. Removing only one face (option B). Both cubes lose the face they are glued on.

In a factory, the number of workers in the assembly, packaging and quality control departments were initially in the ratio 5 : 10 : 8. The factory transferred all the assembly workers and some of the packaging workers to a new facility. After the transfer, the ratio of the remaining packaging workers to quality control workers became 3 : x.

If a total of 108 workers, including 48 packaging workers, were transferred, what is the value of x?

Source: 2026 paper, SNUSAT Q11

How to see it

The transferred total splits into two known parts, and one of them is a whole department — that is what fixes the multiplier k.

Working

  1. Let the departments be 5k, 10k, 8k.
  2. Transferred = 108, of which 48 were packaging, so the assembly department = 108 − 48 = 60.
  3. 5k = 60 ⇒ k = 12.
  4. Packaging left = 10(12) − 48 = 120 − 48 = 72; quality control = 8(12) = 96.
  5. 72 : 96 = 3 : 4, so x = 4.
Answer — B — x = 4.
Common slip. Taking 108 as the packaging transfer, or forgetting that all of assembly went, which is the only thing that lets you find k.

What is the ratio of the four pieces by weight?

You cannot mix a ratio within a part into the ratio of the whole. Rescale so the split part divides exactly.

Working

  2. Now the four pieces are 2, 5, 35/12, 49/12.
  3. Multiply every term by 12: 24 : 60 : 35 : 49.

Tejas works in a logistics company. One day he delivered 10 more than half the packages allotted to him in the morning. After lunch he delivered 15 more than half the remaining packages. If he completed all his allotted deliveries that day, how many packages did he deliver?

Source: 2025 paper, SNUSAT Q12

How to see it

“He completed all of them” is the equation. Everything else is bookkeeping.

Working

  1. Let the allotment be N. Morning: N/2 + 10.
  2. Remaining = N − (N/2 + 10) = N/2 − 10.
  3. Afternoon: ½(N/2 − 10) + 15 = N/4 − 5 + 15 = N/4 + 10.
  4. All delivered: (N/2 + 10) + (N/4 + 10) = N ⇒ 3N/4 + 20 = N ⇒ N/4 = 20 ⇒ N = 80.
  5. Check: morning 50, remaining 30, afternoon 15 + 15 = 30. Total 80. ✓
Answer — B — 80 packages.
Common slip. Reading “half the remaining” as half of the original. That gives 70, which is sitting there as option A.

How to see it

Different roots cannot be compared as they stand. Turn them into powers with one common denominator, or just take logs.

Which of the following could be the breadth and height of the shelf respectively, such that the boxes exactly fit in?

Source: 2026 paper, SNUSAT Q15

Height comes from one box; breadth comes from the total floor the 60 boxes need.

Working

  1. One box: height = volume ÷ floor area = 7·5 ÷ 2·5 = 3 m. No stacking, so the shelf is 3 m high.
  2. Total floor needed = 60 × 2·5 = 150 m².
  3. Shelf floor = length × breadth = 12 × b = 150 ⇒ b = 12·5 m.
  4. So breadth 12·5 m and height 3 m.
Answer — B — 12·5 m and 3 m.
Common slip. Using the shelf volume (60 × 7·5 = 450 m³) and dividing by 12 × 3, which gives the same 12·5 — fine — but reaching for 5 m of height because 450/(12×7·5) = 5.

Same sun, same instant, so height ÷ shadow is a constant.

  2. Tree: h / 28 = 5 / 7 ⇒ h = 28 × 5 / 7 = 20 feet.

21

A survey of 5,000 students in 40 schools, using marks taken from board records rather than from the students themselves, found that students who eat dinner with their family at least five nights a week score on average 12% higher in board examinations. The principal of one school announces: “Family dinners raise examination marks. Every parent should start eating dinner with their child.”

Which statement best describes the logical error in the principal's reasoning?

Source: Predicted 2027, SNUSAT V2

How to see it

The data show an association. A claim that one thing causes another needs the other explanations closed off — and the stem has deliberately closed off every flaw except that one.

Working

  1. The survey is large (5,000 students, 40 schools), so A fails.
  2. The marks come from board records, not from the students, so B fails.
  3. C is not the error: the principal's conclusion is about every parent, and the data are broad enough to describe students in general.
  4. What remains is the jump from goes with to causes: homes where the family eats together nightly may differ in income, routine and parental time, and those could raise marks with or without the dinners. That is D.
Answer — D — correlation taken as causation, with a third factor ignored.
Common slip. Reaching for a generic flaw (small sample, self-report) without checking whether the stem has already ruled it out.

Many cities across India have responded to rising road accidents by imposing stricter traffic fines and vehicle penalties for speeding and rash driving. Traffic authorities argue that heavy penalties deter dangerous behaviour and reduce accident rates quickly. However, a study comparing cities in two states found that accident rates declined most in cities that invested in driver awareness campaigns, improved road design with better signage and safety features, and educated young riders through school-based road safety programmes — even when those cities spent less on enforcement and fines. Penalty-based approaches create anger among drivers, encourage bribery, and fail to address why people drive recklessly in the first place. Approaches such as driver education, community awareness campaigns, and safer road infrastructure address the root causes of accidents and build lasting safety habits.

Which of the following best states the author's main conclusion about reducing road accidents?

Source: 2027 paper, SNUSAT Q12

How to see it

The passage sets two approaches against each other and comes down on one side. The conclusion is that comparison, stated whole.

Working

  1. A says “only” one factor — the passage names three.
  2. C contradicts the passage, which praises driver education.
  3. B is true to the passage but is only half of it: it criticises penalties without saying what works better. It is a premise on the way.

26

Read the following statement.

“If you go outside without a coat, you will catch a cold and infect the rest of the family. Then your sister will have to miss her classes and she will get a bad grade and eventually, fail her course.”

Which of the following fallacies BEST describes the statement above?

Source: 2025 paper, SNUSAT Q4

How to see it

Count the links. A fallacy with a chain of then … then … is almost always the slippery slope.

Working

  1. Lay the statement out as a chain: no coat → catches cold → infects the family → sister misses class → bad grade → fails the course.
  2. That is five links, each asserted as if it were certain, ending somewhere far more serious than where it began. Both features are the signature of the slippery slope.
  3. Check the others do not fit: there is no forced choice between two options (A), no leap from a small sample to a universal rule (B), and nobody's character is attacked (D).
  4. Note that each individual link is merely unlikely; the fallacy is in treating the whole chain as inevitable.
Answer — C — slippery slope.
Common slip. Reading “you will catch a cold” as a hasty generalization. The defect is the chain, not the sample size.

At a parents' meeting, a trustee says: “Either we ban mobile phones from the school building completely, or our students will never be able to concentrate in class. Since we want them to concentrate, a total ban is the only option.”

Which statement BEST describes the flaw in the trustee's reasoning?

Source: Predicted 2027, SNUSAT V1

How to see it

Count the options the speaker allows. When an argument says “either X or disaster” and there are obvious options in between, it is a false dilemma.

Working

  1. The trustee offers exactly two possibilities: total ban, or students who can never concentrate.
  2. Many intermediate policies exist — phones handed in at the start of a lesson, lockers, use only at break — and the argument never rules them out.
  3. That is the defining move of a false dilemma (false dichotomy).
  4. A is ad hominem, C is slippery slope, D is a misplaced appeal to authority; the trustee does none of these.
Answer — B — two extremes presented as the only choices.
Common slip. Picking C because the trustee sounds alarmist. A slippery slope needs a chain of steps; here there is a single either/or.

The most adverse consequences of climate change are not far off. Recent DNA analysis shows that two distinct populations of octopus species, one in the Weddell Sea and the other in the Ross Sea, mated about 125,000 years ago during the last interglacial. This could only have happened if the massive ice sheet (which is West Antarctica) that separates those populations wasn't there at the time. If the South Pole unfroze then, it is very likely to happen this time around too.

Which of the following assertions, if true, strengthens the above argument?

Source: 2025 paper, SNUSAT Q1

How to see it

Split the argument into the historical premise and the present-day prediction, and ask which half still needs help.

Working

  1. Premise: the ice sheet was gone 125,000 years ago. The DNA evidence already establishes that.
  2. Conclusion: it is very likely to happen this time around too — a claim about now.
  3. B reinforces the premise, which was not in doubt, so it adds least.
