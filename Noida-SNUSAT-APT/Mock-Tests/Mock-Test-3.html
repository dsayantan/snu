In a class of 40 students, 22 play chess, 18 play carrom and 7 play both.

How many students play exactly one of the two games?

Source: Predicted 2027, SNUSAT V19

How to see it

Remove the overlap from each group before adding; the overlap is not “exactly one”.

Working

  1. Chess only = 22 − 7 = 15.
  2. Carrom only = 18 − 7 = 11.
  3. Exactly one = 15 + 11 = 26.
  4. 33 is “at least one” (22 + 18 − 7); 7 is the number who play neither as well as the number who play both — neither is asked.
Answer — D — 26.
Common slip. Answering 33, which includes the 7 who play both.

Shown below are four designs. Design P is embedded in either one or more of the other designs. Which of the other designs have P (or a rotated form of P) embedded in them?

Source: 2025 paper, SNUSAT Q19

How to see it

Pick the rarest feature of P and hunt for that, instead of comparing whole pictures.

Working

  1. P is a square carrying three interior lines: a short vertical from the top edge down to a point inside, a line from the left edge meeting that point, and a line from the top of the vertical sloping to the right edge — plus a long line down to the bottom-right corner.
  2. The rare feature is that junction: a vertical stub meeting two slants at one interior point, with a long line running to a corner.
  3. Rotate P by 90°, 180° and 270° and look for that junction in each grid. It is present in Q and in S; R has no vertical stub meeting a slant at an interior point in the required way.
  4. The lines of P must all be present and the angles preserved — a rotation is allowed, a reflection or a rescale is not.
Answer — C — only Q and S.
Common slip. Matching on “busyness”. R is the densest grid and therefore feels most likely to contain anything, which is why it is the distractor.

Aryan is competing in an 800 m cycling race, which consists of two types of sections:

  • Even road: on these smooth stretches Aryan rides at a constant speed of 5 m/s.
  • Uneven sections: each uneven section spans 6 m, and the total time Aryan spends crossing all these sections is exactly 50 seconds.

After every 200 m of the race, the number of uneven sections in the next 200 m is halved compared to the previous 200 m.

If Aryan completes the race in 2 minutes 54 seconds, what is the total number of uneven sections he crossed?

Source: 2027 paper, SNUSAT Q2

How to see it

The total time and the uneven time are both given, so the even-road time follows, and with it the even distance. The halving rule is only a consistency check.

Working

  1. Total time = 2 min 54 s = 174 s. Time on even road = 174 − 50 = 124 s.
  2. Even distance = 5 × 124 = 620 m, so the uneven sections cover 800 − 620 = 180 m.
  3. Number of sections = 180 / 6 = 30.
  4. Check against the halving rule: 16 + 8 + 4 + 2 = 30, so the four 200 m blocks can indeed hold them. ✓
Answer — D — 30.
Common slip. Trying to use the halving rule to find the count. It cannot fix the total on its own; the time data do.

Ravi planned to travel from city X to city Y. For the first 2 hours he travelled at a constant speed. He then increased his speed by 20%, which allowed him to reach his destination 1 hour earlier than originally planned.

What is the total time taken for the journey?

Source: 2026 paper, SNUSAT Q19

How to see it

Only the stretch after the 2 hours is affected, and on it time scales by the inverse of the speed factor.

Working

  1. Let the planned total be T hours at the original speed v.
  2. Planned time for the remaining stretch = T − 2.
  3. At 1·2v that stretch takes (T − 2) ÷ 1·2.
  4. Actual total = 2 + (T − 2)/1·2, and this is T − 1.
  5. (T − 2)/1·2 = T − 3 ⇒ T − 2 = 1·2T − 3·6 ⇒ 0·2T = 1·6 ⇒ T = 8.
  6. The question asks for the time actually taken: 8 − 1 = 7 hours.
Answer — A — 7 hours.
Common slip. Answering 8. That is the planned time, and it is sitting there as option B. Read the last line of the question again before choosing.
5

The sum of the consecutive integers from (−30) to 200 is u.

What is the sum of the consecutive integers from (−200) to 32?

Source: 2027 paper, SNUSAT Q8

How to see it

−30 to 30 sums to zero, so u is really 31 + … + 200. Do the same trick on the second run.

Working

  1. u = (−30 + … + 30) + (31 + … + 200) = 31 + … + 200.
  2. −200 to 32: −32 to 32 cancels, leaving −(33 + … + 200).
  3. 33 + … + 200 = u − 31 − 32 = u − 63.
  4. So the sum is −(u − 63) = −u + 63.
Answer — A — (−u) + 63.
Common slip. Getting the sign of 63 wrong (C). The second run is mostly negative, so its sum must be near −u.

In a factory, the number of workers in the assembly, packaging and quality control departments were initially in the ratio 5 : 10 : 8. The factory transferred all the assembly workers and some of the packaging workers to a new facility. After the transfer, the ratio of the remaining packaging workers to quality control workers became 3 : x.

If a total of 108 workers, including 48 packaging workers, were transferred, what is the value of x?

Source: 2026 paper, SNUSAT Q11

How to see it

The transferred total splits into two known parts, and one of them is a whole department — that is what fixes the multiplier k.

Working

  1. Let the departments be 5k, 10k, 8k.
  2. Transferred = 108, of which 48 were packaging, so the assembly department = 108 − 48 = 60.
  3. 5k = 60 ⇒ k = 12.
  4. Packaging left = 10(12) − 48 = 120 − 48 = 72; quality control = 8(12) = 96.
  5. 72 : 96 = 3 : 4, so x = 4.
Answer — B — x = 4.
Common slip. Taking 108 as the packaging transfer, or forgetting that all of assembly went, which is the only thing that lets you find k.

Anish cut and folded some cardboard to make the container shown. In which of these ways could he have cut and folded his cardboard to make the box?

(The dotted lines represent folds.)

Source: 2025 paper, SNUSAT Q20

How to see it

A net is only a candidate if the face count is right; then check that no two faces would land on the same place.

Working

  1. The container is a hexagonal prism: 2 hexagonal ends and 6 rectangular sides.
  2. (i) A strip of 6 rectangles with a hexagon above and a hexagon below — 6 + 2. ✓
  3. (ii) A central hexagon with 6 rectangles around it and the second hexagon hanging off one of them — 6 + 2. ✓
  4. (iii) The same idea rearranged: a hexagon with 5 rectangles round it, the sixth rectangle carrying the second hexagon and a further rectangle — still 6 + 2, and nothing overlaps on folding. ✓
  5. All three fold up.
Answer — D — all three.
Common slip. Rejecting (ii) and (iii) because they look unfamiliar. A prism has many valid nets; the only disqualifiers are a wrong face count and two faces folding onto the same position.

Which of the following numbers will form a meaningful English word when the display is turned 180°?

Source: 2026 paper, SNUSAT Q12

How to see it

Turning the display upside down does two things at once — it reverses the order and flips each glyph.

Working

  1. Seven-segment digits upside down: 0→O, 1→I, 2→Z, 3→E, 4→h, 5→S, 6→g, 7→L, 8→B, 9→G.
  2. Check the worked example: 0.7734 reversed is 4377.0 → h, E, L, L, O — “hELLO”. The rule is confirmed.
  3. 138 → reverse 831 → B E I — no.
  4. 508 → reverse 805 → B O S — no.
  5. 5537 → reverse 7355 → L E S S — a word.
  6. 7137 → reverse 7317 → L E I L — no.
Answer — C — 5537 reads LESS.
Common slip. Mapping the digits without reversing them. 5537 read left to right gives SSEL, which is nothing — the reversal is half the trick.

The image below shows the side, back and top views of a 3D block structure. Based on the given views, what is the maximum possible number of blocks that can be used to build this structure?

Source: 2026 paper, SNUSAT Q18

How to see it

The top view gives the footprint; the side view caps the height of each column of that footprint. For a maximum, push every column up to its cap.

Working

  1. Top view is 6 × 3, so the footprint is 6 long and 3 deep and every cell is occupied.
  2. Side view is the structure seen along the depth, 6 wide: its column heights read 3, 3, 3, 2, 1, 1, summing to 13.
  3. Back view is 3 × 3 fully filled, so somewhere along each of the 3 depth rows the height reaches 3 — satisfied by the first three columns.
  4. Maximum = make every one of the 3 depth rows match the side view: 3 × 13 = 39.
Answer — A — 39 blocks.
Common slip. Multiplying the bounding box, 6 × 3 × 3 = 54. That ignores the staircase in the side view, and 54 is offered as option D for exactly that reason.

Source: 2027 paper, SNUSAT Q1

What is the final selling price?

Source: Predicted 2027, SNUSAT V20

How to see it

A rise and a fall of the same percentage do not cancel: the fall acts on a larger number.

Working

  1. After the rise: 1,500 × 1.2 = Rs 1,800.
  2. After the discount: 1,800 × 0.8 = Rs 1,440.
  3. Net factor 1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96, a 4% loss on the original.
  4. 1,500 assumes the changes cancel; they do not.
Answer — B — Rs 1,440.

The total surface areas of a hemisphere and a cuboid are s and c respectively. They are joined together as shown below.

What is the total surface area of the solid formed?

Source: 2027 paper, SNUSAT Q9

How to see it

The flat face of the hemisphere sits on the cuboid: that circle disappears from the hemisphere and the same circle disappears from the cuboid's top.

Working

  1. Hemisphere TSA s = 2πr² (curved) + πr² (flat) = 3πr², so πr² = s/3.
  2. Hidden area = πr² (hemisphere's base) + πr² (patch of the cuboid's top) = 2πr² = 2s/3.
  3. TSA of the solid = s + c − ⅔s.
Answer — D — s + c − ⅔s.

Source: 2025 paper, SNUSAT Q12

“He completed all of them” is the equation. Everything else is bookkeeping.

Working

  1. Let the allotment be N. Morning: N/2 + 10.
  2. Remaining = N − (N/2 + 10) = N/2 − 10.
  3. Afternoon: ½(N/2 − 10) + 15 = N/4 − 5 + 15 = N/4 + 10.
  4. All delivered: (N/2 + 10) + (N/4 + 10) = N ⇒ 3N/4 + 20 = N ⇒ N/4 = 20 ⇒ N = 80.

On tapping “P”, the screen displays “A”

If Avinash taps S, E, P, A, L in order, which of the following could his screen display?

Source: 2025 paper, SNUSAT Q11

How to see it

The taps are in a fixed order, so check the options position by position, not as anagrams.

Working

  1. Tap 1 is S, which can show S or P. Tap 2 is E. Tap 3 is P, which can show P or A. Tap 4 is A. Tap 5 is L, which can show L or S.

An ambigram is a word that reads the same when viewed in a mirror (either sideways or upside-down). Dev made a list of ambigrams: WOW, 808, 609, dollop, passed, swims. Of these, he says that WOW and 808 have a special property that sets them apart from the other words in the list.

What is this property likely to be?

Source: 2025 paper, SNUSAT Q7

How to see it

The definition given applies to all six items, so the special property must be something extra. Write each item backwards and compare it with itself.

Working

  1. WOW → WOW and 808 → 808: both are palindromes.
  2. 609 → 906, dollop → pollod, passed → dessap, swims → smiws — none of these reads the same backwards.
  3. So the shared property is being a palindrome, which is what C says.
  4. D is false anyway: 609 has three characters, the same as WOW and 808.
Answer — C — they read the same forwards and backwards.
Common slip. Option B restates the definition of an ambigram, which all six items have. A property that everything on the list shares cannot set two of them apart.
